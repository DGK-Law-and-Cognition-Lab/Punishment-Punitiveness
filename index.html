Overview

Key Findings

Two studies investigate whether the prosocial reasons people invoke when justifying punishment — deterrence, public safety, rehabilitation — are the actual psychological drivers of their punitive choices. We find a divergence between substrate and vocabulary that we describe as a cultural default with selective suppression.

Finding 1
Hostile Aggression Outpredicts Crime Concerns

Punitiveness correlates substantially more strongly with hostile aggression measures (hatred, revenge, degradation, endorsement of suffering; r = .59) than with legitimate crime concerns (r = .32). Steiger's Z = 6.26, p < .001. Robust to three stringency levels of tautology sensitivity.

Finding 2
Prosocial Surface, Mismatched Substrate

Across five classification methods, 71%–96% of justifications get classified as prosocial. But of 33 cells in the text-feature × psychology matrix that reached both significance and a meaningful effect size, 32 were negative — language tracks Study 1 psychology in the wrong direction.

Finding 3
Selective Suppression of Rehabilitation

The most punitive participants do not endorse more revenge or suffering in their language. They specifically drop one prosocial theme — rehabilitation (BGE rehab × punitiveness r = -.37) — while invoking deterrence, incapacitation, and proportional justice at rates indistinguishable from low-hostile participants.

NLP Methods

Participants provided written explanations for their sentencing decisions, analyzed with six computational approaches: sentiment analysis (VADER), dictionary-based classification (Empath + custom dictionaries), zero-shot classification (BART-large-mnli), semantic similarity to prototype sentences (all-mpnet-base-v2), topic modeling (BERTopic), and UMAP visualization with clustering.

Ethics & Limitations

This research was approved by USC IRB (UP-21-00756, exempt status, minimal risk). All participants provided informed consent; no identifying information was collected. Key limitations include correlational design, an online (Prolific) sample, self-report measures (the NLP triangulation does not fully escape this — both modalities are self-generated), single measurement occasion, and three vignettes all involving second-degree murder (generalization to other crime types is an open question).
